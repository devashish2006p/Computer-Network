## What Are Circuit Switching and Packet Switching?

- **Circuit Switching** and **Packet Switching** are not devices or protocols.
- They are **techniques (methods)** used by networks to handle data transmission.
- These techniques decide:
  - Whether a dedicated path is required or not
  - How resources are allocated
  - How data travels from source to destination

---

## 1. Circuit Switching

### What It Is

Circuit Switching is a switching technique in which a **dedicated communication
path (circuit)** is established between the sender and receiver **before**
any data is transmitted.

Once the connection is established, all data travels through the same path
until the communication ends.

---

### How Circuit Switching Works

1. A connection request is sent
2. A dedicated path is established
3. Data is transmitted continuously
4. Connection is terminated after communication ends

---

### Key Characteristics

- Dedicated path throughout communication
- Fixed bandwidth
- Connection-oriented
- Resources reserved even when no data is sent

---

### Real-World Example

- Traditional **telephone (landline) networks**

---

### Advantages

- Guaranteed bandwidth
- No packet loss
- Predictable and continuous transmission

---

### Disadvantages

- Inefficient use of resources
- Connection setup time required
- Not suitable for modern data networks

---

## 2. Packet Switching

### What It Is

Packet Switching is a switching technique in which data is **divided into small
packets**, and each packet is transmitted **independently** over the network.

No dedicated path is required, and packets may take different routes to reach
the destination.

---

### How Packet Switching Works

1. Data is divided into packets
2. Each packet is routed independently
3. Packets may follow different paths
4. Packets are reassembled at the destination

---

### Key Characteristics

- No dedicated path
- Efficient bandwidth usage
- Dynamic routing
- Can be connectionless or connection-oriented

---

### Real-World Example

- **Internet**

---

### Advantages

- Efficient use of network resources
- High reliability
- Scalable and flexible
- Suitable for data communication

---

### Disadvantages

- Packet loss may occur
- Delay and congestion possible
- Requires error handling and reordering

---

## Comparison Summary

| Aspect | Circuit Switching | Packet Switching |
|------|------------------|------------------|
| Path | Dedicated | No dedicated path |
| Resource Usage | Inefficient | Efficient |
| Setup Required | Yes | No |
| Reliability | High | High (with control) |
| Example | Telephone Network | Internet |

---

## Final Summary

Circuit Switching and Packet Switching are **fundamental switching techniques**
used in computer networks. Circuit switching establishes a fixed path for
communication, while packet switching enables flexible, efficient, and scalable
data transmission used in modern networks.
