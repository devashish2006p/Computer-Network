# Network Evolution Timeline

The evolution of computer networks describes how communication systems
progressed from simple point-to-point connections to today’s global
high-speed Internet. This timeline highlights the major milestones in
networking history.

---

## 1. Pre-Network Era (Before 1960)

Before computer networks, communication was done using:
- **Telegraph** – text-based long-distance communication
- **Telephone** – voice communication over wired connections

Computers during this period worked independently and were not connected
to each other.

---

## 2. Early Computing and Time-Sharing Systems (1960s)

- Large mainframe computers were expensive
- Multiple users shared a single computer using **time-sharing**
- This created the need to connect terminals to a central system

This phase laid the foundation for computer networking.

---

## 3. Birth of Computer Networks – ARPANET (1969)

- **ARPANET** was the first computer network
- Developed by the **U.S. Department of Defense**
- Introduced **packet switching** instead of circuit switching
- Initially connected four universities

ARPANET is considered the **origin of the Internet**.

---

## 4. Packet Switching Development (1970s)

- Data divided into small packets
- Each packet travels independently
- More efficient and reliable than circuit switching

Packet switching became the core concept of modern networking.

---

## 5. Development of TCP/IP (1970s–1980s)

- Developed by **Vinton Cerf and Robert Kahn**
- Standardized communication between different networks
- On **January 1, 1983**, ARPANET officially adopted TCP/IP

This event marked the **birth of the modern Internet**.

---

## 6. Expansion of Academic and Research Networks (1980s)

- Universities and research institutions connected globally
- Development of **NSFNET**
- Internet usage expanded beyond military purposes

---

## 7. World Wide Web (1990s)

- Invented by **Tim Berners-Lee**
- Introduced **HTTP, HTML, and web browsers**
- Made the Internet user-friendly

The Internet became accessible to the general public.

---

## 8. Commercialization of the Internet (Mid–Late 1990s)

- Internet Service Providers (ISPs) emerged
- Businesses and e-commerce grew rapidly
- Email and websites became common

---

## 9. Wireless and Mobile Networks (2000s)

- Introduction of **Wi-Fi**
- Mobile networks (2G, 3G)
- Laptops, smartphones, and portable devices connected wirelessly

Networking became mobile and always connected.

---

## 10. Modern High-Speed Networks (2010s)

- Broadband and fiber-optic networks
- Cloud computing
- Social media and streaming platforms
- Data centers and virtualization

---

## 11. Present and Future Networks (2020s and Beyond)

- **5G and upcoming 6G networks**
- Internet of Things (IoT)
- Edge computing
- AI-driven networking

Networks now support billions of connected devices worldwide.

---

> If Circuit Switching or Packet Switching is not clear at this stage, do not worry—both concepts are explained in detail in the next file.


## Summary

The evolution of computer networks progressed from isolated systems to
ARPANET, TCP/IP-based Internet, wireless communication, and modern
high-speed intelligent networks that form the backbone of today’s
digital world.
